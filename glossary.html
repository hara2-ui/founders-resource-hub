Startup Glossary

Speak fluent startup.

The startup world has its own language. This glossary cuts through the jargon with plain, accessible definitions of the terms you will encounter in pitches, investor meetings, and founder communities.

A

Accelerator

Funding

A fixed-term program (typically 3–6 months) that provides early-stage startups with mentorship, resources, and seed funding in exchange for equity. Y Combinator and Techstars are among the most well-known examples.

Angel Investor

Funding

A high-net-worth individual who invests their own money into early-stage startups in exchange for equity or convertible debt. Angels often invest earlier than venture capital firms and may also provide mentorship.

ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)

Finance

The total annualized revenue a company expects from recurring subscriptions or contracts. ARR is one of the most important metrics for SaaS companies and a key indicator of business health and growth trajectory.

Acqui-hire

Exit

An acquisition in which a larger company purchases a startup primarily to gain access to its team rather than its product or technology. The startup's product is often discontinued after the deal closes.

B

Bootstrapping

Funding

Building and growing a business using only personal savings and revenue generated by the business itself — no external investment. Bootstrapped founders retain full ownership but typically grow more slowly than venture-backed companies.

Burn Rate

Finance

The rate at which a startup spends its available cash each month. Net burn rate (expenses minus revenue) determines how long the company can operate before running out of money. Understanding burn rate is critical for financial planning.

B2B (Business-to-Business)

Model

A business model in which a company sells products or services to other businesses. B2B companies typically have longer sales cycles, higher contract values, and more predictable revenue than B2C counterparts.

B2C (Business-to-Consumer)

Model

A business model in which a company sells directly to individual consumers. B2C companies often have shorter sales cycles and lower average transaction values but can scale rapidly with the right product-market fit.

C

Cap Table

Equity

A document tracking who owns equity in a company, how much they own, and what type of equity they hold. A clean, well-maintained cap table is essential for fundraising, investor relations, and eventual exits.

Churn Rate

Metrics

The percentage of customers who stop using a product within a given period. High churn is a signal that a product is not delivering sufficient value to retain users, and it undermines revenue growth.

Convertible Note

Funding

A short-term debt instrument that converts into equity at a future funding round. Convertible notes allow startups to raise capital quickly without immediately setting a valuation — which can be difficult for very early companies.

CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)

Metrics

The total cost of acquiring a single new customer, including all marketing and sales expenses. A healthy business typically has a Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) at least three times its CAC.

M – P

MVP (Minimum Viable Product)

Product

The simplest version of a product that can be released to early customers to test a core hypothesis. An MVP is a fully functional, intentionally limited product designed to validate or invalidate assumptions as quickly as possible.

MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue)

Finance

The total predictable revenue a company expects each month from recurring subscriptions. MRR is one of the most closely watched metrics in SaaS businesses because it directly reflects growth rate and business health.

Pivot

Strategy

A significant change in a startup's product, business model, or target customer based on market learnings. A pivot is not giving up — it is applying what you have learned to a better opportunity. Slack and Instagram both pivoted before finding their winning formulas.

Product-Market Fit (PMF)

Product

The degree to which a product satisfies strong market demand. Founders describe finding PMF as a feeling — users are actively seeking the product, retention is strong, and growth is happening organically. Before PMF, growth should be secondary to learning.

R – S

Runway

Finance

The number of months a company can continue operating at its current burn rate before running out of money. Calculated by dividing cash balance by monthly net burn. Most investors prefer startups to maintain at least 12–18 months of runway.

SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity)

Funding

A legal instrument created by Y Combinator that gives investors the right to receive equity at a future funding round. SAFEs are simpler and cheaper than convertible notes — they have no interest rate or maturity date.

Seed Round

Funding

The first official equity funding round, typically used to build the initial team, develop the product, and find early customers. Seed rounds often range from $500K to $3M and are led by angel investors, seed funds, or accelerators.

SaaS (Software as a Service)

Model

A software delivery model in which applications are hosted in the cloud and accessed via subscription. SaaS businesses are attractive to investors because of their recurring revenue, scalability, and relatively low cost of distribution.

V

Venture Capital (VC)

Funding

Private equity financing provided by venture capital firms to startups with high growth potential. In exchange for capital, VCs receive equity. VC funding typically comes in named rounds — Series A, B, C, and so on — each larger than the last.

Vesting

Equity

The process by which a founder or employee earns their equity stake over time. A standard schedule is four years with a one-year cliff, meaning no equity vests in year one, then equity accrues monthly for the remaining three years.
